# Opportunity Card → Second Surface: Constitutional Reconciliation

**Status:** Analysis for founder ratification. No amendment proposed except where
marked. **Nothing here is established product truth** — the card design under
test is a founder proposal, not a prior constitutional decision.

---

## 1. The card's constitutional job

Under CR-36, the card is a **projection of judgments**. It is never the place
where a judgment is made.

**It may project:**

| Judgment | What the card shows | Layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Verification** | Whether the entity is presently credible | **Entity** |
| **Risk** | Effort and consequence for this person | Pairing |
| **Ranking** | That it is here, in this position | Pairing |
| **Recommendation** | Whether Opportunity X is willing to suggest it | Pairing |
| **Explanation** | Why this, why now | Pairing |

**It must not:**

- Collapse those into one number (**CR-21**, **CR-33**)
- Imply endorsement where there is none (**CR-18**)
- Render a negative judgment more prominently than a positive one (**CR-26**)
- Present a summary that isn't a projection of stored reasoning (**CR-33**)
- Treat a URL or a listing as the opportunity (**CR-35**)

### A requirement the proposal is missing

**The card mixes two layers and must say which is which.**

Verification is a fact about the **entity** — true for everyone (CR-30).
Everything else on the card is about **this person**. A card that presents
*"Verified"* and *"strong fit for you"* in identical visual register implies both
carry the same kind of truth. They do not.

CR-24 requires that a person distinguish what is known from what is inferred.
CR-30 establishes the entity/pairing split. **Nothing currently requires that
split to be visible in the interface** — see §9.

### A state the proposal lacks

R-01 F-6 found misinformation attaching to genuine programmes; R-13 found
closure expressed in none of four topologies. So an entity may carry **claims
that disagree with each other.**

The proposed card has no state for that. *"Verified"* and *"Unverified"* cannot
express *"three sources say open, the official PDF is from 2021, we cannot
reconcile them."* Under CR-18 the opportunity must still surface; under CR-12 the
disagreement must be visible.

## 2. Is "Registration" the right universal concept?

**No. It fails on two independent grounds.**

### It is not universal across the ontology

| Natural act | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Apply** | Scholarship · fellowship · job · internship · programme · accelerator · grant |
| **Register** | Webinar · workshop · conference · training |
| **Enter / submit** | Competition |
| **Sign up** | Volunteering |

"Register" is natural for roughly four of thirteen. Naming the surface
*Registration* generalises the **webinar term** across scholarships and
fellowships, where it reads as a category error. The founding episode was a
scholarship; one does not *register* for it.

### Naming a surface after its terminal action creates conversion pressure

This is the stronger objection, and **it comes from your own audit criteria.**
You listed *"the Registration surface becoming a conversion funnel"* as a failure
mode to guard against.

A page called **Registration** has registration as its purpose. Everything above
the action becomes preamble to it. Under pressure, the explanation compresses to
get people to the form faster — which is **CR-33's hollowing**, arriving through
the name rather than through neglect.

Compare what CR-12 says the surface is actually for: *reducing uncertainty*. The
page exists so a person can understand what pursuing this involves — including
deciding **not** to pursue it. A surface that has done its job when someone walks
away informed cannot be named after the act of signing up.

### What follows

The **final action must be contextual** — Apply · Register · Enter · Submit ·
Join · Attend — which your proposal already has right. The **surface** should be
named for the person's question, not the system's conversion.

Candidates, none of which I'm selecting for you:

- **"What this involves"** — names the CR-12 function directly
- **"Pursue"** — the universal act under the ontology; a possibility is *pursued*
- **Unnamed** — the card expands into the full opportunity surface with no label

*"Details"* is correctly rejected: it invites the information dump. But the
alternative isn't the terminal action's name. **Genuine founder decision. Not
resolvable from the Constitution.**

## 3. Does the attention/action separation follow from the Constitution?

**Yes. It is derived, not invented.** CR-25 already sets the hierarchy:

> Awareness → Ranking → Explanation → Recommendation → **Decision by the person**

The card occupies **Ranking + Recommendation**. The second surface occupies
**Explanation → Decision**. The split you proposed is the constitutional sequence
projected into two surfaces.

**One correction.** The card is not reasoning-free. CR-33 makes summaries
*projections of stored reasoning* — so the card is literally that projection.
*"Why this deserves your attention"* on the card must be generated from the same
reasoning artifact the second surface expands, never written separately. If the
card's line and the full explanation can drift apart, CR-33 is already violated.

## 4. Information hierarchy

### On the card

| Element | Layer | Constitutional basis |
|---|---|---|
| Title, provider, type | Entity | CR-35 — the entity, not a listing |
| **Verification state, with recency** | **Entity** | CR-11 — decays, so a bare badge is wrong |
| Status + deadline, marked **derived** | Entity | R-01/R-13 — expiry is never read, always inferred |
| Key eligibility signal | Pairing | CR-32 — circumstance, never capability |
| **Why this deserves your attention** | Pairing | CR-12, CR-33 — projection of stored reasoning |
| Why now | Pairing | CR-08, CP-C — runway, not just deadline |
| Effort signal | Pairing | CR-29 — consequence-proportionate |
| One salient fact | Entity | Discretionary |

### On the second surface

Everything CR-12 enumerates: why it exists · who it is for · why the person
matches · **why they do not** · required documents · selection process · hidden
expectations · common mistakes · estimated effort · probability bands ·
alternatives — plus preparation (CR-09) and the legitimate participation route
(FPR-01).

Plus all six judgments **individually inspectable** (CR-21) and CR-33's six
reasoning components: known · inferred · evidence · uncertain · why surfaced ·
why ranked here.

### Never, anywhere

- A single composite score standing for several judgments (**CR-21**)
- *"95% match"* as the reason (**CR-12**, **CR-33**)
- A verification badge without recency (**CR-11**)
- *"Don't apply"* or *"not worth your effort"* (**CR-25**)
- Capability inference — *"people like you rarely succeed here"* (**CR-32**)

## 5. Interested — semantics

**Records:** a declared relationship between a person and an **entity** —
*"preserve this possibility for me."*

**May influence:** retrieval of the person's own saved set · timing and reminders
(CR-08) · the person-model **where the person can see and edit it** (CR-24).

**May never:**

- Become an engagement metric (**CR-04**)
- Enter ranking as a behavioural signal (**CR-06**, guard **G2**)
- Have its **absence** read as disinterest (**CR-31**)
- Narrow what the person subsequently sees (**CR-31**)

### The tension, and its resolution

If Interested cannot influence ranking, it is inert. If it can, it looks like the
behavioural signal CR-04 forbids.

**Resolution: Interested is a declaration, not a behaviour.** An explicit,
inspectable, editable statement by the person is categorically different from an
observed click. CR-24 makes it visible in the person-model; CR-31 forbids reading
its absence. A click is inferred; a declaration is stated — and the Constitution
already distinguishes those.

**Consequence:** Interested must appear in the person-model as a stated fact the
person can remove. If it exists only as an event in a log feeding a recommender,
it has become behavioural profiling wearing a button.

## 6. Keeping the six judgments separate

The card projects conclusions; the judgments stay independently addressable
(**CR-21**, guard **G5**). Practical test: **for any card, can each of the six
judgments be retrieved separately, with its own evidence?** If the card renders
from one blended object, CR-21 is nominal.

## 7. Ontology compliance

The card represents an **Entity**, never a claim and never a URL (CR-35). It is
assembled from N observations resolved to one entity (CR-36), and must be able to
express:

- *"Evidenced by several sources"* — provenance
- *"Sources disagree"* — the missing state from §1
- *"Last confirmed X ago"* — CR-11 decay, surfaced not hidden

## 8. Compliance-shaped failure audit of the proposed card

| Failure mode | Risk | Observable test |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation → generic summary | **HIGH** | Distinct-reasoning ratio across cards. Identical phrasing rates. |
| **Verification → static badge** | **HIGH — present in the proposal.** *"Credibility: Verified"* is a badge, and CR-11 says verification decays. | Does every card's verification carry a recency? Count badges without one. |
| Recommendation hidden in a score | **LOW** — the proposal explicitly rejects *"95% match"* | Any single number standing for multiple judgments |
| Negative judgment visually stronger | **MEDIUM** — *"Effort: Substantial"* as a chip is visually loud; CR-26 governs delivery, not only content | Compare visual weight of positive vs negative elements |
| Interested → engagement metric | **MEDIUM** | Does Interested appear in the person-model as editable, or only in an event log? |
| Second surface → conversion funnel | **HIGH — the name causes it.** See §2 | Ratio of explanation to action affordance; does the page succeed when someone leaves informed? |
| Closed/uncertain looking actionable | **HIGH** — R-01/R-13: closure is expressed by no source | Are *"closed"* and *"we cannot confirm it is open"* visually distinct? |
| Empty state treated as failure | **MEDIUM** | Is CR-20's *"nothing worth your attention"* a designed state, or an error screen? |

## 9. Is an amendment needed?

**Almost entirely no.** The Constitution already determines the card's job, the
attention/action split, the information hierarchy, and Interested's semantics.
The proposal is largely a correct reading of existing law.

**One candidate, offered for your judgment rather than asserted:**

CR-30 splits entity truth from pairing judgment, and CR-24 requires known and
inferred to be distinguishable. Neither requires that split to be **visible in
the interface**. A card showing *"Verified"* beside *"strong fit for you"* in the
same register implies equivalent epistemic status for a fact about the world and
an inference about a person.

Whether that needs a rule, or is adequately covered by CR-24 read broadly, is a
founder decision. **I am not proposing wording.**

## 10. Proposed flow

### Card — *"Why should I give this my attention?"*

```
[Title]                                    [Type]
[Provider]

Open · closes 14 Oct · derived, last confirmed 2 days ago
Nigerian undergraduates, final year

WHY THIS DESERVES YOUR ATTENTION
[specific reasoning, projected from the stored artifact]

Why now: [runway, not merely deadline]
Effort: Moderate                Verified · 2 days ago

[ Interested ]              [ What this involves → ]
```

**Projects:** ranking · recommendation · verification (entity, labelled) ·
risk/effort · explanation.
**Never:** a composite score · a bare badge · a discouraging verdict.

### Second surface — *"What does pursuing this actually involve?"*

Why this exists → who it's for → why you match → **why you might not** →
requirements → what it will cost you → how selection works → hidden expectations
→ common mistakes → alternatives → what to prepare → **legitimate route to
participate.**

**Terminal action is contextual:** Apply · Register · Enter · Submit · Join ·
Attend.

**The surface succeeds when the person can decide — including deciding not to
pursue.** That is the test that keeps it from becoming a funnel.

| Contextual terminal action | *Apply now* as a universal |
| *(surface name — founder decision)* | **Registration** as the surface name |
| *(surface name — founder decision)* | **Details** |
