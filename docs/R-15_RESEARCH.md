# R-15 — Institutional Coverage of Opportunity Announcement

**Question:** What proportion of opportunities reaching Nigerian students are
announced by at least one enumerable institution?

---

## The headline is methodological: I will not give you a percentage

**The denominator cannot be established by desk research, and any number I
produced would be an artifact of method rather than a measurement.**

The corpus below was assembled by searching the web. That biases it fatally in
two directions:

1. **Circularity.** Several entries were found *by domain-filtering searches to
   university sites.* Anything found that way is class A **by construction**. It
   cannot be evidence about coverage.
2. **Survivorship.** Opportunities that never entered a search index are absent
   entirely — and that is precisely the population most likely to be class B.

*"Opportunities reaching Nigerian students"* and *"opportunities indexed on the
open web"* are different populations, and this method can only sample the second.
A coverage figure derived from it would be **circular in the numerator and
truncated in the denominator.**

So: no percentage. What follows are **structural findings**, which survive the
sampling bias because they concern existence rather than proportion.

## Structural findings

### F-1 — Institutional announcement is not confined to large organisers · **B+**

**ZEANI corrects R-14's model.** The Zion Empowerment Arena for the Needy
Initiative is a small NGO running a secondary-school scholarship — about as
sub-institutional as an organiser gets. **University of Nigeria Nsukka announced
it on its own site.**

R-14 concluded the variable was *organiser weight*. ZEANI shows that is too
crude. A small organiser that **routes** its opportunity to an institution whose
community it affects gets institutional announcement.

**The refined variable is routing, not weight:**

> An opportunity acquires an institutional announcer when it is routed to an
> institution whose community it affects — whether by the organiser approaching
> the institution, or by the institution's own responsibility for its people.

Large organisers route by default, being institutions themselves. Small ones
route when they ask. **DCP is peculiar precisely because it originated *inside* a
university and still produced no institutional announcement found.**

### F-2 — No category is structurally excluded · **B+**

Every category the founder listed has at least one institutionally-announced
instance in the corpus:

scholarship · fellowship · internship · competition · grant/programme · training ·
workshop · volunteering · accelerator

**This is the strongest finding that survives sampling bias.** It does not
establish a rate, but it does establish that **no category systematically lacks
institutional announcers.** Webinars remain the thinnest — represented only by
workshops and forums.

### F-3 — Universities announce three distinct kinds · **B+**

1. **Their own** — CB4EE workshop, Young Innovators Forum, volunteering
2. **External opportunities affecting their students** — PTDF, TAGDev, NELFUND
3. **Third-party opportunities routed to them** — ZEANI

Kind 3 matters most. It is the mechanism by which small organisers enter the
institutional record, and it is why mechanism 5's ceiling is higher than R-14
implied.

### F-4 — Subdomain versus new domain is a discoverability fault line · **B+**

| Opportunity | Portal | Reachable by monitoring the announcer? |
|---|---|---|
| Wema Hackaholics | `hackaholics.wemabank.com` | **Yes** — subdomain of a permanent monitored domain |
| 3MTT | `3mtt.nitda.gov.ng` | **Yes** — subdomain of an agency domain |
| NELFUND | own gov domain, announced by others | **Yes** — via announcers |
| **DCP** | **`ulesarb.org`** | **No** — independent registrable domain, no announcer found |

**Well-resourced organisers put new programmes on subdomains of domains they
already own.** Those are reachable by monitoring the parent. An independent new
domain with no institutional announcer is the actual blind spot — and it is
narrower than "any new domain."

### F-5 — Third-party application platforms are a fifth topology · **C**

NLNG's scholarship runs applications through `candidate.scholastica.ng`. The
claim and the application route live on different domains, owned by different
parties. Not enough evidence to characterise; noted for R-13's topology list.

---

## Coverage ceiling

**Not measurable from this corpus. What can be said:**

- Institutional monitoring reaches **every category tested**
- It reaches opportunities from **every organiser class tested**, including a
  small NGO
- It reaches **new publishers** through old announcers (NELFUND, 3MTT, Hackaholics)
- One case in twenty resisted it, and that case is **atypical** — a
  university-internal society whose own university appears not to have announced it

The evidence is **consistent with a high ceiling and a bounded residual.** It
does not measure either.

---

## Consequence for C-18

**Narrow again. Still do not close.**

R-14 scoped C-18 to *sub-institutional organisers*. **ZEANI shows that is still
too broad** — a small NGO obtained institutional announcement. The residue is
narrower:

> **C-18, third scoping:** opportunities whose organiser neither *is* an
> institution nor *routes* to one, published on an independent domain with no
> institutional announcer. DCP is the only instance in this corpus, and its
> failure to route despite originating inside a university suggests the class may
> be small and partly idiosyncratic.

**Not a structural hole on this evidence.** But the residue remains unmeasured,
and *unmeasured* is not *small*.

---

## Consequence for the Engine of Discovery

**Mechanism 5 is confirmed as the primary discovery layer.** It reaches every
category and every organiser class tested, and it catches new publishers via old
announcers.

Two refinements:

1. **Monitor subdomains of enumerated announcers, not just their pages.** F-4
   shows new programmes commonly appear at `programme.institution.tld`. A
   monitor watching only known page paths would miss them; one watching the
   domain's subdomain space would not.
2. **Universities are the highest-value announcer class** because they carry all
   three kinds — including third-party opportunities routed to them, which is the
   only observed path by which small organisers enter the institutional record.

---

## Next unresolved Phase 14 question

**R-15 cannot be completed by desk research, and that is the finding.**

The denominator requires a sample of opportunities drawn from *what actually
reached students* — which can only come **from students.** Any web-derived corpus
is biased by construction, and no amount of further searching fixes it.

> **R-16 — Assemble a corpus of opportunities that demonstrably reached real
> Nigerian students, sampled independently of web discoverability, and establish
> for each whether an enumerable institutional announcer existed and how early.**

Method: ask students what opportunities they heard about in the last twelve
months and where they first heard of them. Then trace each backwards to its
earliest institutional signal, if any.

**This is the first question in the entire project that requires contact with
real users rather than analysis.** It is worth naming as a milestone: desk
research has now taken the Engine of Discovery as far as it can go, and the
remaining uncertainty is empirical in a way only fieldwork resolves.

Everything else in Phase 14 is either answered or architecture.
